# Job Hunting OS Guide - Start Here

A reusable job-search kit for working with an AI assistant. Start with the guided setup, then choose the working threads and supporting tasks that help your search.

**Supporting files:** [Workspace Map](Workspace-Map.md) · [Blank Tracker](Job-Hunting-OS-Tracker.xlsx)

**Navigate:** [Initial setup](#section-i-initial-setup) · [Build your file map](#4-build-your-file-map-with-your-assistant) · [Working threads and tasks](#section-ii-continue-your-search-with-working-threads-and-supporting-tasks) · [Verify your setup](#18-check-that-your-setup-works)

Guide version 0.8 | Repository package 0.3 | October 18 2026. This package follows the current Guide 0.8; the companion tracker contract remains template 0.3. Copying these files installs no tasks and changes no account settings. Independent-candidate testing remains pending.

Your own folder holds your career materials, tracker and working documents. Your AI assistant helps review your experience, establish a search direction, find and assess opportunities, develop relationships, prepare for interviews and keep records current. Where direct access or automation is unavailable, each workflow has a manual path.

## Section I: Initial Setup

### 1 Create a folder for your job search

In Google Drive, choose New, then New folder. Name the folder Job Hunting OS.

This is where you will keep your own job-search documents and spreadsheet. If your AI assistant has permission and the tools to create a folder, you can ask it to do this for you. You can also use another storage service. The important thing is to have one place where you can find your files again.

### 2 Get your two supporting files

This repository has three files: this README guide, [Workspace-Map.md](Workspace-Map.md), and [Job-Hunting-OS-Tracker.xlsx](Job-Hunting-OS-Tracker.xlsx). Use your own private copies for your search.

Download the tracker workbook using GitHub’s download button. Upload it to your own Google Drive folder, open it in Google Sheets and choose File → Save as Google Sheets if it opens in Office editing mode. Keep the converted workbook in your folder and name it Job Hunting OS Tracker. You can also use the downloaded workbook in Excel. Check its eight tabs, dropdowns and Dashboard formulas in your chosen application before relying on it.

Open Workspace-Map.md and copy its text into a document in your folder named Job Hunting OS Workspace Map, or download and edit a private Markdown copy. It can remain blank initially; the assistant helps fill it in during Step 4. Markdown is plain text with formatting, so a code editor or Git installation is not required.

Save or download this README as well if your assistant cannot access the repository. Make it available as Job Hunting OS Guide - Start Here, or record its actual README filename in the map. Keep versions inside the files, not in their filenames. Keep completed maps, personal records and career materials out of the public repository.

### 3 Add your resume, profile and other files to your AI workspace

Add in your resume and LinkedIn profile even if they need updating. This will help us set up the system and you will get the opportunity to update your resume and profile before you start using the OS.

- Put your latest resume in the Job Hunting OS folder.

- Add a PDF or export of your LinkedIn profile to the folder, or create a document containing your headline, About section and work history and save it there.

Next, make these 5 job-search files available to your AI assistant. If your assistant supports projects or shared workspaces, create one called Job Hunting OS. Upload files or connect them from your storage service using the options available in your assistant to this project/workspace.

For example, in ChatGPT:

- Create a project called Job Hunting OS.

- Open the project’s sources area. If your version shows Project settings → Available sources → Add, use that option.

- Add your own copies of these five files:

- Your current résumé.

- Your LinkedIn profile text or export.

- Job Hunting OS Guide - Start Here, this README or your saved private copy, which includes the prompts and task instructions.

- Job Hunting OS Tracker, your copied spreadsheet.

- Job Hunting OS Workspace Map, which can still be blank.

- Optionally if you already have achievement notes, project summaries or other materials that explain your experience, add them too. If you don’t, that’s fine. Your assistant will help you develop them through conversation.

PS: If your AI project/workspace does not support adding files, we will add the files directly when we start our chat with the AI Assistant.

In Step 4, your assistant will check what it can actually read and help you fill in the Workspace Map.

### 4 Build your file map with your assistant

Your Job Hunting OS Workspace Map is a simple document showing where your job-search files are saved. You and your assistant will create it together.

Open a new chat inside the project you created in Step 3, or a standalone chat if projects are unavailable, and name it Job Hunting OS Setup and Weekly Review. Paste this PROMPT to get going:

```text
Help me set up my Job Hunting OS. Start by helping me build my Workspace Map through conversation.
Check which project files you can actually read, including Job Hunting OS Guide - Start Here, my résumé, LinkedIn materials, Tracker sheet and Workspace Map. Tell me what you can access and help me supply anything essential that is missing. Do not assume a link gives you access.
Read my Workspace Map template first. Follow its existing sections, field names and order. Fill in information already supported by my answers and accessible files, then ask for anything missing, one useful question at a time. Do not invent file locations or ask me to complete the template on my own.
Mark optional materials Missing and tools we have not configured Not set up. If I do not have achievement notes or project summaries, leave those entries Missing for now.
If you can edit my copy of the map, show me what you plan to enter and ask whether I want you to save it. After saving, check the result. Otherwise, give me completed text using the same headings, field names and order as my Workspace Map template, ready to paste into my copy.
Once the map is usable, guide me to the next step in this guide. Continue in this same chat without restarting setup.
```

You are ready to continue when the map identifies the files you have and clearly marks anything missing. You do not need every file ready, email connected or scheduled tasks configured.

### 5 Review your résumé and build your experience notes

Continue in the Job Hunting OS Setup and Weekly Review chat from Step 4. Your assistant will help review your résumé and LinkedIn materials and capture the experience behind them.

You do not need to prepare achievement notes or project summaries yourself. Paste this prompt into the same chat:

```text
Now help me review my career materials and build a useful record of my experience.
Use the Workspace Map we just prepared to find my current résumé, LinkedIn materials and any existing achievement notes. Confirm what you can actually read.
Ask whether my résumé and LinkedIn profile are up to date and whether I am satisfied with them. Use my answer to decide where to begin. If either is missing, help me build it.
Read the available materials before asking questions. Check for missing recent work, unclear responsibilities, inconsistent facts and accomplishments that need stronger evidence.
Help me capture my experience through conversation, one relevant project or accomplishment at a time. Ask what needed to change, what I personally did or led, the decisions and tradeoffs I made, what happened and what I learned. Ask one useful question at a time and avoid repeating information already established.
Use my answers to draft achievement notes and project summaries. Distinguish my personal contribution from team results. Do not invent metrics or turn an observed improvement into a proven causal claim. Help me identify what can be shared publicly and what should remain confidential.
Show me proposed notes and résumé or LinkedIn changes for review. Save agreed changes in my own files when editing is available and authorized, then verify them. Otherwise, provide ready-to-paste text. Add any newly created files to my Workspace Map. Do not claim my live LinkedIn profile changed unless that change is verified.
Keep this focused. We do not need to document my entire career before continuing. Once my materials are usable and we have captured a few relevant accomplishments, summarize remaining gaps and guide me to the next step in this same chat.
```

You are ready to continue when your résumé reflects your current experience, your LinkedIn materials are consistent with it or have clear pending corrections, and you have a small set of reviewed accomplishment notes. You can build on these throughout your search.

### 6 Roles: Decide what you want to pursue

Continue in the Job Hunting OS Setup and Weekly Review chat. Your assistant will help turn your experience, interests and practical needs into a search direction.

Together, you will prepare two short documents and save them in your Job Hunting OS folder:

- Job Hunting OS Candidate Profile: your professional background, strengths and practical circumstances.

- Job Hunting OS Search Strategy: the opportunities you want to pursue, your selection criteria and a manageable search rhythm.

Your assistant will add their locations to your Workspace Map and help you add them to your AI project’s sources where supported.

You do not need to know your ideal job title or have every answer ready.

Paste this prompt into the same chat:

```text
Help me decide what opportunities to pursue, using the career materials and experience notes we just reviewed.
Use my Workspace Map to find the current materials and any existing Candidate Profile or Search Strategy. Confirm what you can read. Build on information already established and ask one useful question at a time.
Start by asking what I want from my next opportunity, what I enjoyed in previous work and what I would like to change.
Explore relevant role families, responsibilities, seniority, industries and employment types. Help me consider adjacent possibilities supported by my experience without assuming my next role must match my last title.
Clarify my location, remote or hybrid preferences, travel limits, compensation needs, timing and any eligibility requirements. Separate hard constraints from preferences. Keep unknowns explicit rather than treating them as reasons to reject an opportunity.
Explain the Priority choices already defined in my Tracker, with a simple example of each. Help me agree on what makes an opportunity worth pursuing, what counts as a concrete concern, and when to keep something on watch. Save my agreed criteria in the Search Strategy. Keep Priority separate from fit, pursuit decision and the order in which I should act.
Ask how much time and energy I can realistically give the search each week. Help me choose a manageable balance between finding roles and developing relationships.
Read the Tracker’s How This Works instructions, current headers and allowed values before proposing priority, pursuit or ranking rules. Explain the existing choices in plain language and agree with me on how to use them. Do not invent dropdown values, change the schema or turn one company-specific decision into a global exclusion.
Prepare or update two concise documents for my review:
Job Hunting OS Candidate Profile: summarize my profession, relevant background, verified strengths, interests and practical circumstances that matter to the search. Reference my career evidence rather than duplicating every accomplishment.
Job Hunting OS Search Strategy: summarize the work and role families I want to pursue, why they fit my experience, hard constraints, preferences, open questions, priority and pass criteria, application-queue ranking rules, and a realistic weekly search rhythm.
Keep named company watches, contacts, applications and dated outcomes in their current owning tracker records. Do not embed them as permanent instructions in these documents.
Show me the proposed profile and strategy, including unresolved questions, and review them with me.
Save the agreed documents in my own Job Hunting OS folder, using the names Job Hunting OS Candidate Profile and Job Hunting OS Search Strategy. Find the folder through my Workspace Map. If these documents already exist, update the current copies rather than creating duplicates.
If editing is available and authorized, save the changes and verify the content and folder location. Otherwise, provide the completed text for each document and guide me through creating or updating the files and saving them in that folder.
Add the actual file locations to my Workspace Map, or give me exact entries to paste if you cannot edit it. Help me add the documents to my AI project’s sources where supported. Do not report files as saved or available to the project until confirmed.
Keep this manageable. Treat the strategy as a starting direction we can refine through experience. Once it is usable, summarize our decisions and remaining questions, then guide me to Step 7 in this same chat to begin finding and recording opportunities. Do not restart setup.
```

You are ready to continue when you have a reviewed Candidate Profile and an agreed Search Strategy saved in your Job Hunting OS folder, with their locations recorded in the Workspace Map, or an explicit saving blocker. Open questions can remain open; you do not need a perfect strategy before beginning.

### 7 Tracker: Build your first tracker records with the assistant

This step starts your opportunity records. Your assistant will help you record any opportunities already underway, find or assess a few roles, and choose useful next actions. It will store these opportunities in the tracker sheet you have in your folder. From hereon we will call the tracker sheet as the Tracker.

Your Tracker contains eight tabs, which are separate sheets within the same spreadsheet:

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Dashboard | A calculated summary of recorded activity and current progress. |
| Roles | Jobs you are considering or pursuing, including existing applications. |
| People | Relevant contacts, relationships and conversation history. |
| Target Companies | Employers you want to consider or monitor. |
| Networking Events | Events you are considering, registered for or have attended. |
| Activities & Follow-ups | Planned actions, due dates and completed activity. |
| Interviews | Interview dates and references to preparation and debriefs. |
| How This Works | Field definitions and rules for maintaining the Tracker. |

Continue in the Job Hunting OS Setup and Weekly Review chat.

Paste this prompt into the same chat:

```text
Help me begin using my Job Hunting OS Tracker, following my saved Candidate Profile and Search Strategy.
Find my copied Tracker through the Workspace Map and confirm what you can read and edit. Read its How This Works instructions and follow its field definitions and recording rules. Check existing records before adding anything, preserve supported information, and ask me when a required fact is unclear. Explain rules when they affect a decision rather than walking me through every field upfront.
Check that my copied Tracker is ready to use, including its date formats, dropdowns and dashboard defaults. Use the timezone recorded in my Workspace Map to check its timezone setting. Make any needed setup changes within my authorization and verify them. If you cannot change a setting, explain the exact action I need to take.
First ask whether I already have applications, recruiter conversations or upcoming interviews that need recording. Help me capture those accurately using the information and evidence I have. Leave unknown facts explicit.
Next, ask whether I have a specific posting to assess. If not, help me find one to three current opportunities using my Search Strategy.
Confirm whether you can search the web or use connected job-search tools. If available, search using my agreed role families, seniority, location, employment types and hard constraints. Check promising results against employer career pages or official application sites where accessible.
For each opportunity, show the title, company, posting link, why it fits, concrete concerns and anything you could not verify. Review the shortlist with me before entering agreed opportunities into the Tracker.
If live search is unavailable, help me construct searches I can run myself and ask me to share promising posting links or text. Do not invent vacancies or present remembered listings as current.
Record people directly involved in these opportunities when relevant. We will build my broader relationship circle in a separate conversation; I do not need a contact at every company to begin.
Choose one to three practical next actions with me. Give each a reason, a small first step and an appropriate due date, then prepare the corresponding Activities & Follow-ups entries.
Show me the proposed tracker changes. If editing is available and authorized, check the current affected records, save the agreed changes and read them back to verify the exact saved values. Otherwise, give me exact entries grouped by tab and field, help me paste them into my copy, and check the saved rows I share.
Draft messages and applications for review. Do not send or submit them without explicit authorization.
Keep this manageable. Fewer than three roles or actions is fine, and empty tabs are fine. Finish by explaining what was saved, what remains proposed or blocked, and guide me to the next step in this same chat.
```

The Dashboard summarizes what you and your assistant record in the Tracker. Your assistant will help you interpret it as your search develops.

You are ready to continue when the opportunities you agreed to track and a few useful next actions are saved and checked, or any blockers are clearly identified. You can continue even if no suitable new role was found.

### 8 People: Build your initial relationship circle

Continue in the Job Hunting OS Setup and Weekly Review chat. Your assistant will help you identify people you could comfortably approach for a conversation, advice or support.

Start with people who know you and your work. You do not need to know whether they have a job opening.

Together, aim to identify around 15 people, then choose three for your first conversations. A smaller starting group is fine. You will not be asked to message everyone at once.

Paste this prompt into the same chat:

```text
Help me build my initial relationship circle for my job search, using my saved Candidate Profile and Search Strategy.
Read my current People records and the Tracker’s How This Works instructions. Check what connection lists or correspondence I have provided or authorized you to access. Do not assume you can see my LinkedIn connections or earlier conversations.
Build the shortlist with me through conversation. Start by asking about former colleagues, managers, collaborators, friends or other professional contacts who know my work and whom I would feel comfortable approaching for help. Ask one useful question at a time. If I struggle to recall names, use my career history to suggest groups of people to think about, without inventing individuals or relationships.
Aim for around 15 promising people, but work in small batches and let me start with fewer.
For each person, help establish how we know each other, how well they know my work, our last meaningful interaction and why reconnecting could be useful. Consider relationship strength, career alignment, relevant perspective or connections, timing and a natural reason to reach out.
Use the relationship-strength categories already defined in my Tracker. Explain them as needed and discuss uncertain classifications with me. Keep relationship strength separate from current priority: a close relationship may not need immediate outreach, while a new contact may be relevant to an active opportunity.
Show me the proposed shortlist with a brief rationale and possible ask for each person. Do not rank people solely by seniority or assume willingness to help.
Help me select up to three people for the first round. Favor genuine, approachable conversations. An appropriate ask might be a catch-up, feedback on my positioning, market insight or an introduction. Do not force a job or referral request.
For each selected person, check prior outreach where available, do only the research needed, and draft a concise message in my voice with one clear, proportionate ask. Review the drafts with me. Do not send without explicit authorization or repeat outreach that is already awaiting a response.
Show me proposed People and Activities & Follow-ups entries. Follow the Tracker’s recording rules, preserve existing history and save agreed changes when authorized, then verify them. If editing is unavailable, provide exact entries to paste and help me check the saved result.
Record sending, replies, conversations and completed assistance only when supported by evidence or my confirmation. Agree on follow-up timing rather than assigning arbitrary deadlines.
Finish with a manageable first outreach plan. Explain that future reviews will balance new outreach against progressing existing conversations and commitments. Three new outreaches in a week can be a starting rhythm, not a quota.
```

You are ready to continue when you have an initial shortlist and a small, agreed outreach plan. Messages can remain drafts.

Progress means useful conversations, learning, introductions and opportunities. Not every relationship needs to lead directly to a job.

### 9 Choose your next action and establish your rhythm

You now have the foundations of your search: career materials, a search direction with the roles you want to pursue, an initial relationship circle of people you want to work with, and a working tracker to organize everything.

You do not need to finish every setup detail before taking action.

Now, let's continue in the Job Hunting OS Setup and Weekly Review chat to choose what matters next and decide how you want to keep working.

Paste this prompt into the same chat:

```text
Help me finish the initial setup and choose my next useful action.
Review the files and records we have prepared. Briefly summarize what is saved and verified, what remains a draft, and any missing information or access blockers. Do not restart the completed setup or make me repeat established answers.
Ask how much time and energy I have available. Help me choose one to three next actions across my current opportunities, relationship outreach and preparation needs. Prioritize anything time-sensitive. For each action, explain why it matters and give me a small, concrete first step.
Check existing Activities & Follow-ups before proposing new entries. Save agreed actions when authorized and verify them, or provide exact entries for me to paste. Do not send messages, submit applications or schedule meetings without explicit authorization.
Help me choose a realistic time for a weekly review. That review should consider current opportunities, conversations, commitments, upcoming interviews, learning and capacity, then select the next few useful actions. Agreeing on a review time does not create a reminder or scheduled task.
Explain the ongoing options in this guide:
Keep using this chat for planning and weekly reviews.
Use a dedicated working conversation when a topic needs more focused attention.
Set up an optional recurring task when repeated discovery or checking would genuinely reduce work.
Recommend only what would help me now. Do not require me to create every conversation or enable every task. If I choose one, guide me to the relevant prompt in this guide.
Save a short continuation note in my Job Hunting OS folder when authorized, or provide ready-to-save text. Include my current file and rule references, the next agreed action, unfinished setup and any access limitations. Point to the live tracker for people, applications and outcomes rather than making the note a competing record. Add its location to my Workspace Map.
Finish by telling me the single best action to start with and how to return for my next review.
```

Initial setup is complete when you can take your next action and know how to return. You can keep improving your materials, expanding your network and refining your strategy as you learn.

The following sections provide working-conversation prompts and optional recurring tasks. They are tools to use when helpful, not another setup checklist.

## Section II: Continue your search with working threads and supporting tasks

In Steps 1–9, you prepared your career materials, search direction, tracker and initial relationship circle. You will now use working threads, dedicated conversations with your assistant, to continue your search.

Each thread focuses on an area such as Roles, People or Target Companies. Use it to discuss findings, make decisions, prepare materials and keep the relevant tracker records current.

Tasks support these working threads. Where automation is available, tasks can run searches and checks on a schedule you choose, then save or report findings for use in the relevant thread. For example, a Roles task can discover opportunities that you review and pursue in your Roles working thread.

| Area | What you do in the working thread | What a supporting task can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roles | Assess opportunities, tailor applications and manage your pipeline. | Find matching opportunities. |
| People | Develop relationships, choose outreach and draft personalized messages. | Review relationship progress and suggest next steps. |
| Target Companies | Decide which employers to monitor and explore relevant paths. | Check watched companies for developments and openings. |
| Networking Events | Assess events, decide whether to attend and plan follow-up. | Find relevant events. |
| Activities & Follow-ups | Manage commitments, deadlines and next actions. | Surface upcoming or overdue actions, including interview preparation. |
| Interviews | Prepare, practice, debrief and capture learning. | Upcoming preparation needs are included in the Activities & Follow-ups review. |
| Dashboard | Review progress and choose priorities across your search. | Prepare a search summary and proposed priorities. |
| Email | Review correspondence, draft responses and reconcile developments with your tracker. | Check authorized email and record supported updates across the relevant tabs. |

The threads and tasks use the same files and Tracker. Their names match the tracker topics wherever possible. Email supports several tabs rather than having its own tab.

If automation is unavailable, you can run the same searches and checks manually with your assistant in the relevant working thread. You can also supply posting links, messages or other information when direct access is unavailable.

Below, we’ll provide the complete prompts and a recommended setup, so you can get straight to using them. Each topic will have the same simple structure:

- Working thread: exact chat name and a complete prompt to paste.

- Supporting task: exact task name, recommended schedule and a complete task prompt.

- Manual option: how to run that task in the working thread if automation is unavailable.

The prompts will use the Workspace Map and saved Search Strategy and we’ll cover Roles, People, Target Companies, Networking Events, Activities & Follow-ups, Interviews, Dashboard and Email. Interviews will have a working thread, with reminders handled by Activities & Follow-ups.

For each topic below, create the named working thread and paste its prompt. Then use the supporting task prompt to enable scheduled help, or run it manually in that thread. Start with Roles and People; add the others when useful.

### 10 Roles

Use this working thread to assess opportunities, decide what to pursue, prepare applications and maintain your Roles tab. Its supporting task searches for opportunities you can review in this thread.

#### Create your working thread

Open a new chat inside your Job Hunting OS project, or a standalone chat with the current mapped files if projects are unavailable. Name it Roles and paste this prompt:

```text
Be my job-search partner for Roles. Help me discover and assess opportunities, prioritize what deserves attention, prepare applications and maintain accurate Roles records.
Use my Workspace Map to locate my Candidate Profile, Search Strategy, approved résumé, career evidence, Tracker and current workflow rules. Confirm what you can actually access. Continue from saved setup without asking me to repeat established information.
Read How This Works, current headers, allowed values and the complete records needed for decisions and deduplication. Current records govern pipeline state; reliable original evidence establishes events. Resolve conflicts without overwriting newer information.
Search sources
Read the Search Sources section of my Search Strategy. If it is missing, help establish it using sources relevant to my profession, seniority, location and employment types. Show me the proposed list before saving it in my Job Hunting OS Search Strategy.
For technology-oriented searches, possible starting sources include public LinkedIn Jobs, Built In, Wellfound, Levels.fyi Jobs, AI-Jobs.net, Hired, We Work Remotely, Remote OK, Hacker News Who Is Hiring/jobs, Y Combinator Work at a Startup, and Go Fractional for relevant fractional work. These are starting candidates, not a guarantee that every service remains available or suitable. Verify current relevance and accessibility.
Include official employer careers pages, credible recruiters and executive-search firms, and employer application systems such as Workday, Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, iCIMS, SmartRecruiters and Breezy HR. Adapt the list for other professions.
Record source locations, relevance, access limitations and any candidate-selected exclusions in Search Sources. Keep named-company monitoring membership in Target Companies. Do not hard-code individual companies or contacts into permanent instructions.
Assessing opportunities
Assess postings I supply and findings from supporting searches. Evaluate actual responsibilities, ownership and requirements rather than title or keywords alone.
Apply hard constraints and pass criteria before assigning priority. Separate eligibility, narrative fit, priority and application action order. Missing information is a verification question, not automatically a concern or lower priority. Explain any concrete concern under my configured definitions.
Use official employer or application-site evidence where accessible. Preserve the actual discovery source separately from the authoritative job link. Keep unknowns and access limitations explicit. An inaccessible posting does not prove closure.
Match by employer, requisition or job identifier, canonical URL and role scope. Detect reposts and aggregator aliases without merging genuinely distinct openings. Keep anonymous recruiter opportunities separate until their identity is verified. Preserve confidentiality.
Follow the Tracker’s priority and ranking contract. Rank only eligible pre-application opportunities, using numeric, unique and contiguous ranks when required. Clear rank after application or other departure from the queue without hiding active interview or follow-up needs. Do not create a Fit Score or restore removed fields.
Applications and warm paths
For promising roles, check current People records and verified hiring-team paths. Confirm current affiliation before describing someone as an insider. Coordinate outreach through saved People and Activities records without assuming another chat is accessible. Do not delay a time-sensitive application indefinitely while waiting for help.
Warm Path / Contact contains actual people’s names only, separated by semicolons, or is completely blank. Store explanations and evidence in Relationship, Notes or linked Activities. Keep general Person IDs separate from warm-path qualification.
Before application, a plausible role-specific person path may be recorded. When recording application submission or moving to Applied, revalidate each listed person. Use reliable existing evidence; otherwise ask: Did [person’s name] actually help with this application through a referral, introduction, internal outreach, recruiter engagement, or another concrete action?
After application, retain only verified concrete help for that opportunity: a completed referral or introduction, internal outreach on my behalf, routing my information internally, material recruiter engagement advancing the process, or comparable assistance creating access or internal visibility. An interview is not required.
Identification, connection acceptance, unanswered outreach, uncompleted offers, pending introductions or referrals, employment at the company, or being named on the posting do not establish qualifying help. Remove unverified names while preserving history. Later evidence can restore a name.
Apply this standard to later milestones and terminal outcomes. Preserve legitimate helpers after rejection or closure. Do not infer an application from a pre-application closure. Warm-path-only updates never change hiring stage or its date.
Prepare tailored materials using approved career claims. Distinguish my contribution from team results and do not invent metrics. Draft applications and messages for review; do not submit or send without explicit authorization.
Recording and next actions
Record applications, interviews, outcomes and assistance only from reliable evidence or my confirmation. A draft, alert or silence does not establish an application, referral or rejection.
Keep posting availability, hiring milestone, process outcome and fit/pursuit decision separate. Preserve actual application dates and evidence after later outcomes. Change the stage date only when the corresponding hiring stage changes; research, outreach, verification and Notes edits do not reset it.
For changes I request or authorize, reread affected records immediately before writing, make bounded changes and read back every affected record to verify exact saved values. Preserve schema, formulas, history and unrelated fields. Check warm-path values and qualifying evidence after Roles updates. Report verification gaps honestly.
If editing is unavailable, provide exact entries grouped by tab, record identifier and field, and help me verify the saved result.
Keep named people, company watches, applications and dated outcomes in current records. Never make a decision solely because an old example, prompt, calibration or conversation named someone.
Include the employment types in my Search Strategy. Keep fractional or advisory job opportunities in Roles; use a separate prospect or business-development record only when that work becomes an active conversation or prospect.
Finish broad reviews with at most three practical actions. Put agreed actions in Activities & Follow-ups. Say when no opportunity qualifies.
Start by reading my current Roles records, checking whether Search Sources is established, and identifying what deserves attention now.
```
